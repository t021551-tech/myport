ظلّ — عربات مكيفة تنقل زوار المباركية

Al-Mubarakiya is a walking market — narrow lanes, a gold souq at one end, the date sellers at the other, and about four hundred metres of hot ground in between. Zill is a fleet of twelve enclosed, air-conditioned golf carts that shuttle shoppers from one souq to the next: you wait in a misted shelter, a cart pulls up at 22 °C, and you step out three minutes later at the spice lane instead of arriving at it soaked.

02

The cart

العربة

A stock six-seat electric golf cart cannot hold 22 °C in a Kuwaiti August — an open canopy has nothing to cool. Zill's cart is the closed version of the same vehicle: hard doors, laminated glass, an insulated roof, and a roof-mounted 12,000 BTU DC air-conditioner sitting over its own inverter.

Specification

Design targets for the August case — the hardest day of the year.

  • Seats6 passengers + driver
  • Cabinenclosed, 22 °C setpoint
  • Air-conditioner12,000 BTU / 3.5 kW cooling
  • AC electrical draw0.5–1.4 kW with heat
  • Battery48 V · 210 Ah · 10 kWh LFP
  • Shift on one pack4.5 h in August, 8 h in January
  • Charge2.5 h at 4 kW, at the hub
  • Speed in the lanescapped at 12 km/h
  • Doorshard, with laminated glass
  • Accessible carts2 of 12, ramp + securement
  • Footprint2.4 m × 1.2 m

✶Pre-cool on the charger

The pack that cools an empty cabin from 48 °C is a pack that isn't moving anyone. Carts pull the cabin down to 22 °C while still plugged in at the hub, so the first ride of a shift starts cold on mains power, not on the battery.

التبريد المسبق أثناء الشحن

✶Doors closed, always

Half the cooling load in a shuttle is the door standing open at a stop. Doors are power-operated, open for the boarding count and shut on their own — and the roof unit steps to high for ninety seconds after each stop to recover the loss.

أبواب تغلق تلقائيًا

✶Shelters do the first ten degrees

Stepping from 48 °C into 22 °C is a hard jolt. Each stop is a shaded, misted shelter sitting around 10 °C below the street, so the step into the cabin is the second half of the drop, not all of it.

مظلات مرذوذة في كل محطة

✶Narrow enough for the lanes

At 1.2 m wide the cart clears the covered lanes with room for people to pass, and it is speed-capped to 12 km/h with a walking-pace limiter in the crowded stretches. The perimeter streets are the only place it runs at 20 km/h.

03

Why any of this

لماذا التبريد؟

Kuwait has some of the hottest summer afternoons on Earth — July and August routinely run past 45 °C, and the country holds one of the highest temperatures ever reliably recorded (53.9 °C at Mitribah, July 2016). Al-Mubarakiya keeps trading through it, which is why it empties out between noon and late afternoon and refills after sunset.

04

How it would run

التشغيل

Hours

08:00–13:00, then 16:00–23:00 — the souq's own rhythm. Thursday and Friday evenings run the full twelve carts; a quiet Tuesday noon runs four. In Ramadan the fleet moves to a single night shift, roughly 19:00 to 02:00.

Fare

250 fils flat anywhere inside the loop, 500 fils to the outer car parks, paid by KNET or contactless at the door. Free for passengers over 65, children under 6, and anyone using a ramp cart — the fare is there to keep carts moving, not to price out the people who need them most.

Dispatch

Nearest free cart by lane distance, not straight line. If no cart is within four minutes the shelter screen says so honestly instead of counting down a number it can't keep. Carts with under 15% pack return to the hub and are replaced from the two spares.

Drivers

Twelve carts, sixteen drivers across two shifts, trained on the lane speed limiter and on heat-illness signs — the driver is the person most likely to notice a passenger in trouble before the passenger does. Every cart carries chilled water and a first-aid kit.

Where the carts sleep

One hub on the Mubarak Al-Kabeer edge: twelve 4 kW chargers, a shaded canopy, and a wash bay. Charging is deliberately slow and overnight on the cheap side of the load curve, with opportunity top-ups between rounds during the afternoon lull.

What it is not

Not a tour, not a taxi out of the souq, and not a replacement for walking Al-Mubarakiya — the walking is the souq. It exists for the 400 m in the middle of a 48 °C afternoon that stops an older shopper, a pregnant woman or a family with a pram from making the trip at all.
