05
  • 06 SEP 2026

    Why train/test splits exist

    • ML basics

    Confused me: if the model scores 99% on the data I gave it, why is that a bad sign rather than a good one?

    Clicked when: someone described it as marking your own homework with the answer sheet open. The score isn’t wrong — it’s just not measuring the thing you care about, which is what happens on data it’s never seen.

  • 31 AUG 2026

    SQL joins, properly this time

    • SQL

    Confused me: LEFT vs INNER. I’d been picking whichever one returned a number that looked about right.

    Clicked when: I stopped thinking in Venn diagrams and started asking “do I want to keep rows that have no match?” If yes, LEFT. That’s the whole decision.

  • 24 AUG 2026

    Accuracy is a bad metric more often than not

    • ML basics
    • Changed my mind

    Confused me: a fraud model at 99.5% accuracy sounded excellent until I checked what it was predicting.

    Clicked when: I worked out that guessing “not fraud” every single time also scores 99.5%. Precision and recall stopped being exam vocabulary at that exact moment.

  • 17 AUG 2026

    pandas: groupby is just a pivot table

    • Python

    Confused me: the syntax, mostly. Split-apply-combine sounded far more complicated than what it does.

    Clicked when: I rebuilt a pivot table I’d already made in Excel and got the same numbers out. Group by the column you’d drag into rows, aggregate the one you’d drag into values.

  • 10 AUG 2026

    What a token actually is

    • AI

    Confused me: I assumed tokens were words, then couldn’t work out why Arabic text cost so much more than English for the same paragraph.

    Clicked when: I ran both through a tokeniser and watched the counts. Tokens are chunks the model was trained to recognise, and languages with less training data get chopped much finer.
