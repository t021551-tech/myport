01

Kuwait Towers

أبراج الكويت

◇

Ras Al-Ardh, Sharq · Arabian Gulf Street

Drag to turn · 187 m, 147 m, 113 m41,000 enamelled discs

Three towers, opened in 1979, and the only building most people can draw from memory. The tallest carries two spheres: the lower one holds a water reservoir and a restaurant, the upper one is a viewing floor that turns a full circle while you sit in it. The second tower is water storage. The third has no sphere at all — it lights the other two.

The blue-green skin is the part photographs never quite catch: roughly 41,000 enamelled steel discs in eight shades, set so the spheres change colour depending on where the sun is. Iraqi forces damaged the towers during the occupation; they were repaired and reopened in 1992, then closed for a long restoration and reopened again in 2016.

Go about an hour before sunset. You get the city in daylight, the turn of the viewing floor through dusk, and the Gulf lit up on the way down.

02

Mirror House

بيت المرايا

◇

Qadsiya · by appointment only

Drag to turn · the street wall and façadeMosaic laid 1972–2006

A family home in a quiet street where every wall, floor, ceiling and door is covered in hand-cut mirror mosaic. The Italian-born artist Lidia Al-Qattan, wife of the Kuwaiti painter Khalifa Al-Qattan, worked on it from 1972 to 2006 — more than thirty years of one continuous piece.

It is still a private house, not a public museum, and that is exactly what makes the visit what it is: you are let in, given tea, and walked through room by room. Set aside about two hours. Nothing here is behind glass.

Call ahead — there is no turning up. Confirm the tour and the current price when you book.

The Maritime Museum

المتحف البحري

◇

Arabian Gulf Street, Sharq

Drag to turn · the gallery and its dhowsDhows & pearling

If you only understand one thing about Kuwait before oil, make it this: the country lived off the sea. This museum is where the whole pearl-diving world is laid out properly — the boom and sambuk hulls, the diving ropes and stones, the turtle-shell nose clips, and the merchants’ brass sieves for grading a pearl by the hole it falls through.

It is small, and that works in its favour. An hour here changes how you read every other place on this page, including the towers.

Do this one first if you can. Everything else on the coast makes more sense afterwards.

04

The Scientific Center

المركز العلمي

◇

Salmiya · Arabian Gulf Street

Drag to turn · the sail canopies at nightOpened 2000

Opened in 2000, and still the best answer to a hot afternoon. The aquarium is the reason to come — one of the largest in the region, and it is built around Gulf habitats rather than a generic tropical tank, so you get the mudflats and the desert side of the coast too. There is also a large-format dome cinema and a hands-on Discovery Place for younger visitors.

Outside on the water sits a restored wooden dhow in its own harbour. Go and stand next to it: the scale of what men sailed to the pearl banks in is not something you get from a photograph.

Weekday mornings are calm. Weekend afternoons are not.

05

Kubbar Island

جزيرة كبر

◇

~30 km offshore · boat only, ~40 min

Drag to turn · the cay and its reefReef at 4–14 m

A low sandy island with a ring of coral around it, roughly 30 km off the southern coast and about forty minutes out by boat. The water sits between 4 and 14 metres, with most of the reef around nine down — hard and soft coral, ledges, reef fish, and turtles if the day is good to you.

There is nothing on the island. No shade, no water, no shop. Everything you need goes in the boat with you, and everything you bring comes back out with you.

Morning, not evening — the water is clearer and the anchorage empties out. Cooler months only; summer here is punishing.

06

Khiran Resorts

منتجعات الخيران

◇

Al-Khiran · ~100 km south of Kuwait City

Drag to turn · the resort strip: block, lawns, beachModelled from the resort

The strangest thing on this list, and the most Kuwaiti. South of the city, where the desert met the Gulf, engineers cut a lattice of channels straight into the sand and let the seawater in — creating around 200 km of artificial shoreline where there had been almost none, and a chalet on the water for people who grew up wanting exactly that.

It is a weekend place: chalets, a marina, resorts, and long quiet water that stays flat when the open Gulf is choppy. Come for a night rather than a day — it is a hundred kilometres each way.

Rent early for the cool months. Chalets on the water go long before the season starts.

07

Failaka Island

جزيرة فيلكا

◇

~20 km offshore · ferry from Ras Al-Ardh

Drag to turn · the excavated groundDilmun under Hellenistic

The only Kuwaiti island with a real history of settlement, and it has several layers of it stacked on top of each other. Bronze Age Dilmun sits under a Hellenistic Greek fortress — Alexander’s people called the island Ikaros — and above both is a modern village that was emptied during the occupation and largely left as it stood, buildings still marked.

That last part is why the island stays with people. You walk from a 4,000-year-old foundation to a house with 1990 still written on the wall of it, in the same afternoon.

Check the ferry timetable before you plan anything else — the last boat back decides the whole day.

08

Dickson House

بيت ديكسون

◇

Arabian Gulf Street, Kuwait City

Drag to turn · white volumes, blue screensLived in until 1990

One of the last old seafront houses still standing on the Gulf Road, now a cultural centre. It was home to Harold and Violet Dickson — he was the British political agent, she stayed on in Kuwait for decades after him, right up to 1990 — and it is furnished as a house rather than staged as a museum.

The value of it is the ordinary detail: rooms, courtyard, wind direction, how a family actually lived on this shoreline before the towers went up across the road.

Twenty minutes on foot from the Maritime Museum. Do the two together.

Center of Manuscripts, Heritage and Documents

مركز المخطوطات والتراث والوثائق

◇

Jabriya · researchers, by arrangement

Drag to turn · the display room~12,000 manuscripts

Not a day out — a working archive, and the quietest place on this list. It holds on the order of 12,000 filmed manuscripts of Arab and Islamic heritage, collected and indexed from libraries around the world, along with historical documents and its own published bulletins.

If you study anything — history, language, law, Islamic sciences — this is a resource sitting in Jabriya that most people in Kuwait have never walked into.

Contact them before you go and say what you are looking for. Reading rooms like this run on appointments, not walk-ins.

10

Al Qurain Martyrs Museum

متحف شهداء القرين

◇

Qurain · the house, kept as it was

Drag to turn · 24 February 1991Preserved, not repaired

On 24 February 1991, nineteen members of the Al-Messilah resistance group were in this house when an Iraqi patrol approached. What followed was a ten-hour battle between lightly armed young men and armour. Twelve of them were killed. Seven survived beneath the rubble.

The rubble was cleared and nothing else was. The walls stayed as the fighting left them — pitted with small-arms fire inside and out, with the cavities where shells came through still open. It was made a museum by Amiri decree and opened in 2003.

Give it the quiet it asks for, and read the names before you leave. This is not a photo stop.

Kuwait Police Museum

متحف الشرطة

◇

Bneid Al-Gar · off Arabian Gulf Street

Drag to turn · the entrance and its signMuseum since Feb 2014

Opened as a museum in February 2014 in an older building near the Gulf Road. Inside: uniforms from the 1930s onward, service weapons, and a small crime lab with a fingerprinting kit and a forensic entomology section. Outside: a line of retired patrol cars, oldest to newest, which is the part everyone photographs.

It is free, it is rarely busy, and it tells a version of the country’s last ninety years that no other museum here covers.

Split opening hours — typically mornings and again in the evening, closed Friday. Confirm before you drive over.

12

Al Shaheed Park

حديقة الشهيد

◇

Kuwait City · on the line of the old city wall

Drag to turn · shell canopy, museum bar, rib roofModelled from the park layout

Kuwait’s largest urban park, opened in 2015 and laid out along where the old city wall once ran — which is why the shape of it is a long band rather than a square. Lawns, a lake, running paths, an amphitheatre, and two museums built into the landscape rather than sitting on top of it: a habitat museum and a memorial museum.

It is the one place in this group you can go to without a reason. Walk the loop after dark, when the city towers are lit behind the ridge line.

Evening, always. Bring someone and do one full circuit before you sit down.

13

Kuwait Geopark

الحديقة الجيولوجية

◇

North of Kuwait Bay · Jal Az-Zor & Subiya

Drag to turn · the gateway, cut in strataJal Az-Zor & Subiya

North of the bay, the flat country finally breaks. The Jal Az-Zor escarpment runs for more than 60 km above the shoreline, rising to around 150 m, cut by wadis and folded by the tectonics that made it — and out towards Subiya the ground goes strange enough that people describe it as Martian.

It is being developed as Kuwait’s geopark, with the aim of eventually joining the UNESCO Global Geoparks network. The same area holds settlement traces going back to the Ubaid period, roughly 5500–4900 BC.

Access is the hard part: much of it needs prior permission, a 4×4, and someone who knows the tracks. Arrange it before you set out, and never go alone.
