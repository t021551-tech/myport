What this is honest about

Four promises, and one thing we refuse to pretend.

One ID number. Nothing else. No name, no phone number, no address, no email. Your ID number is what lets a doctor legally prescribe to you and stops the same controlled prescription being issued twice — it goes to your prescriber and to nobody else. Everywhere else on the service, including the doctor’s queue, you are an alias.
Your family cannot be notified. There is no next-of-kin field. Only you can add a person, and only you can remove them — except in an overdose escalation, which is spelled out on the band page.
Nothing leaves this page. Nothing is kept after you close the tab. This page makes no network requests at all — no fonts, no analytics, nothing. Your plan is held for this tab only and your ID number is never written to the device. Two things it cannot clear for you: the address in your browser history, and anything a shared device has synced. On a shared device, use a private window. Leave now, top right, erases what is stored and sends the browser to an ordinary page immediately — so does pressing Escape three times.
Telling us you used is not punished. An honest report reaches your doctor as a note, never an alarm, and never resets your plan to day one. Hiding it is what the band is for.
The band detects drug use from your body signs.

Seven signals, sampled continuously: heart rate, heart-rate variability, blood oxygen, breathing rate, skin temperature, sweat, and tremor and movement. Each drug class moves that set of seven in its own direction, and the combination is what identifies it — an opioid drops breathing and oxygen while the pulse slows, a stimulant drives the pulse up and flattens recovery while the skin heats and sweats. Rafeeq compares the live pattern against your own seven-day baseline, names the class it matches and how strong the match is, and sends it to your doctor the same minute. Confirming which drug it was, and what to do about it, is your doctor’s call — the band is what tells them to make it.

Outside 9am–5pm, go to a door rather than a phone. For wanting to die, for hearing or seeing things, or for a withdrawal that has turned frightening, the address above is the right door. For an overdose, a seizure, chest pain, or someone who cannot be woken, call 112 and go to the nearest general hospital emergency department instead — those are medical emergencies, and a general A&E is faster at them than a psychiatric hospital. Signs of an opioid overdose — breathing slow or stopped, lips or fingertips blue, cannot be woken: give naloxone if you have it, call 112, stay until help arrives. Nobody needs your name to keep you alive.

        After the plan ends

        Clean is not the finish line. It is the point where most services stop paying attention, and this one does not.

        Relapse risk is highest in the first ninety days and stays raised for a year. That is not a warning about your character — it is what the numbers do, for everybody. So the band stays on, the doctor keeps a slot, and a psychologist keeps checking the part of this that was never about the drug. You can end any of it whenever you want. Until you do, it continues by default, because the day you feel finished is exactly the day this tends to come back.

        The psychologist

        Somebody whose whole job is the part that was never the drug.

        Most people who get clean and relapse do not relapse because the drug won. They relapse because the thing underneath — a depression, an anxiety nobody named, a trauma, an undiagnosed ADHD, a grief — is still there and the drug was the only thing answering it. A psychologist is on your team from week two for that reason, not as a reward for good behaviour.

        Relapse prevention, in the specific weekly at first, then monthly Your three real triggers by place and hour, and what you do instead — written down while you are well, not improvised at 2am.
        Screening for what was underneath day 14, week 6, then every 6 weeks in year one Depression, anxiety, PTSD and ADHD are all common under long substance use, all treatable, and all missed when everyone is watching the drug.
        Trauma work, when you are ready for it not in the first month Opening this too early destabilises people. A good psychologist will tell you to wait, and that is competence, not avoidance.

        Giving the hours back

        Volunteering in Kuwait — because empty time is the thing that beats people.

        Using takes up hours. Getting clean hands them back all at once, and an empty Thursday is more dangerous than a craving. Work that is needed, on a schedule somebody else depends on, is one of the few things that reliably fills that space — and being useful to a community you were hiding from does something to shame that no session can. These are real Kuwaiti organisations that take volunteers.

          Two rules before you sign up. Do not take a role that puts you near your substance — not a pharmacy stockroom, not a hospital drug cupboard, not a bar shift, however much they need the help. And do not mentor other addicts in your first year: peer work is genuinely powerful and it is also the fastest way back for someone still finding their own footing. A year of stability first, then it is one of the most useful things you will ever do.

          Treated outside Kuwait

          Leaving the country is a real option. So is doing the paperwork at 2am from your bed.

          Plenty of people will accept treatment abroad who will not walk into a clinic ten minutes from their family’s house. That is not weakness and it is not vanity — in a country this small, being recognised in a waiting room is a real cost. Here are the two routes, with the privacy of each stated honestly.

          Route one

          A private clinic abroad, paid for yourself

          Who in Kuwait knowsNobody. No ministry file, no medical committee, no employer, no relative signing anything. You book it, you fly, you come back. This is the private route, and it is private because you are paying for it.
          What it costsMoney, and all of it. Residential rehab abroad is priced per week and the number is large. Ask for the total before you send a deposit, and ask what the number excludes — detox, medication, and aftercare are often separate lines.
          What it is good forDetox and the first weeks, away from the people and places attached to using. Distance genuinely helps at the start.
          What it cannot doCome home with you. Recovery is decided in the months after you land, in the same city you left, which is why the aftercare question below matters more than the brochure.
          Names, not recommendations
          Every hospital below is a real, established place that treats addiction, listed so you have somewhere to start reading instead of a search engine full of paid adverts. None of them is endorsed here, none has been inspected by anyone connected to this page, and prices are not quoted because they change. Check each one yourself on the register named beside it — and against the Ministry of Health’s own approved-hospital list if you are going through the funded route. The nearest four are Arabic-speaking, which for most people matters more than the mountains.
          • National Rehabilitation Center (NRC) Abu Dhabi, UAE · government · nrc.gov.ae Set up in 2002 and a WHO Collaborating Centre for substance-use treatment and prevention. The most credentialed addiction service in the Gulf, an hour and a half’s flight away.
          • Erada Center for Treatment and Rehabilitation Dubai, UAE · government · check: DHA Sheryan directory Detox, residential care for around 50 inpatients, and a two-year outpatient aftercare programme — the longest aftercare on this list. Reported as men only; confirm when you call.
          • Al Amal Hospital Dubai, UAE · government · check: DHA Sheryan directory Dubai’s public psychiatric hospital, working alongside Erada for addiction and for the psychiatric side of it.
          • Masar Center Jeddah, Saudi Arabia · private · check: Saudi MOH licence Describes itself as the first comprehensive mental-health and addiction centre in the Kingdom and the Gulf. Arabic-speaking, and close.
          • Castle Craig Hospital Peeblesshire, Scotland · private · check: Care Inspectorate An addiction hospital since 1988, with medical detox on site and a 12-step programme supplemented by CBT, DBT and EMDR. Rural, residential, far from anyone who knows you.
          • Priory Roehampton, London, and 500-odd UK sites · private · check: CQC Britain’s largest private mental-health and addiction provider. Quality varies by site, which is exactly why you read that site’s own CQC inspection report before booking.
          • Clinic Les Alpes Montreux, Switzerland · private · licensed by the Swiss health authority The top of the price range: Forbes reported programmes starting around US$49,000. Included here so the range is honest, not because expensive means better.
          • The Cabin Chiang Mai, Thailand · private · check: Thai Ministry of Public Health licence Inpatient and outpatient treatment for substance and behavioural addictions. Far cheaper than Europe, and far from everything, which cuts both ways.
          • Hazelden Betty Ford Foundation 15 centres, United States · non-profit · check: state licence & CARF/Joint Commission Non-profit, founded 1949, and the place most of modern addiction treatment was written at. Visas make it the slowest option from here.

          Egypt, Jordan and Lebanon all have addiction hospitals, and Kuwaitis do use them, but the ones circulating online could not be verified to the same standard as the nine above — a current licence, a regulator, or a named accreditation. Ask the Treatment Abroad Office for its approved list rather than trusting a directory that sells placements.

          Before you pay anyone, anywhere

          Six questions, and the register to check

          Is it registered, and where can I see that? England: CQC · Scotland: Care Inspectorate · international: JCI Every serious country publishes a searchable register with inspection reports. If a clinic cannot give you its registration number, that is the answer.
          Is the detox doctor-led, on site? not "medically supervised" — a doctor, in the building For alcohol, benzodiazepines, pregabalin and sleeping tablets this is the whole question: withdrawal from those can cause seizures.
          What happens if I need a hospital at 3am? named hospital, distance, who travels with me A villa with a nurse on call is not an answer.
          What is the total, and what is excluded? in writing, before a deposit Medication, detox, family sessions and aftercare are the usual extras.
          Who prescribes for me when I get home? a named doctor in Kuwait, arranged before you fly back A clinic abroad generally cannot lawfully prescribe to you in Kuwait once you leave.
          Will they write to that doctor? discharge summary, medication list, in English or Arabic Without it your Kuwaiti doctor is starting from nothing.
          The part people get caught by: bringing the medication home. Controlled medicines — buprenorphine, methadone, benzodiazepines, strong painkillers, some sleeping tablets — cannot simply be posted into Kuwait or carried in loose. They need proper documentation, and the rules are enforced. If a clinic abroad stabilises you on something, settle who continues it in Kuwait, and on what paperwork, before you fly home. Coming back to nothing is how a good month ends badly. The weeks after you land are also when relapse risk peaks — and after a clean stretch, your old dose can stop your breathing.

If keeping this from your family is the thing you cannot compromise on, route one is the honest answer and route two is not.

          Eligibility for the funded route is a question for the Treatment Abroad Office, and reporting suggests it is aimed at citizens: confirm your own entitlement on 2481 0931 rather than assuming either way. Whether addiction treatment specifically is approved for funding depends on the committee’s finding that it is not available in Kuwait — and it largely is available here, free, which is the argument the committee will make. Expect that, and go in with a reason it does not fit your case.

          The third option people forget

          Staying here, and being treated privately

          Kuwait has private psychiatrists who treat addiction, and the government service at the address on the front page is free for citizens and expatriates alike. Neither one calls your family. Leaving the country is a way to buy distance from the people around you — it is not the only way to buy privacy, and it is the most expensive one.

            • ✓Price, and the date
            • ✓The handle or number the seller used, if the patient has it

            Why it is built this way, and not the obvious way. A treatment service that hands patient records to the police is a treatment service nobody uses, and the people it loses are exactly the people with something to hide — which is all of them. Kuwait’s own 2025 drug law makes the same bet: Article 61 files no case against someone who comes forward for treatment first. So the patient side stays sealed, and what crosses the wall is supply-side description that no single patient can be identified from.

            Sellers are not caught by one person’s testimony anyway. They are caught by the same area, the same method and the same packaging arriving from twenty people who have never met. That is a pattern, and a pattern needs no names.
