Where the wall sits

The patient record

  • patients — alias, a hash, last four digits
  • care_team — the only route a clinician gets in
  • plans, plan_progress
  • bands, band_readings, detections
  • alerts, phq9_responses, patient_messages

The supply side

  • supply_reports — governorate, method, description, handle, price, time
  • drug_control_staff — empty, and no INSERT policy
  • supply_clusters — the view above

0 columns naming a patient or user. 0 foreign keys into anything on the left.

The two sides are not separated by a policy that could be edited later. There is no column to join them on, which is a different kind of promise.

Constraints that carry an argument

civil_id_hash ~ '^[0-9a-f]{64}$' — only a hash fits the column. Storing a raw Civil ID is impossible, not discouraged.
confidence <= 0.97 on detections — body signs identify a class, not a molecule. A readout printing 100% would get a patient accused over a fever.
item9 generated from the answers — the self-harm item is pulled out on purpose, so it can route to crisis help before a score is ever shown.
status = 'returned' — not "relapsed". A return resumes the plan at the phase that fits.
Every clinician in this dataset has licence_verified = false, and the one unverified crisis number is flagged in the data rather than displayed. Nothing here is cleared to treat a real person.
