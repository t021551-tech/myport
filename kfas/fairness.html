Fairness report

Fairness you can check, not assume

Nothing on this page is entered by hand. Every figure is computed from the interview records — the questions actually asked, the scores actually given and the evidence actually cited — so changing a score on a review page moves these numbers.

What the record shows

    Did every candidate get the same questions?

    A core question that was never put to a candidate is a criterion scored on nothing. This is the check that catches it.

    Panellist calibration

    How far each panellist sits from the panel’s own average on the same answer. A steady gap in either direction is a person to calibrate, not a candidate to re-score.

    Who was doing the talking?

    A candidate who spoke for less than half of their own interview was not given the same chance to show evidence as one who spoke for three quarters of it.

    Did the time of day matter?

    Mean weighted score by interview slot. A steady fall across the day is panel fatigue, not candidate quality.

    Where the scores sit

    Every individual score given in this round, 1 to 5. A panel that only ever uses 3 and 4 is not discriminating between candidates.

    What this page cannot tell you

    Being straight about the limits is part of the method:

    • These are small numbers. A gap across six interviews is a prompt to look, not proof of bias.
    • Nothing here measures outcomes by gender, nationality or age. That needs diversity data collected separately, with consent, and reported in aggregate — never shown next to a named candidate.
    • A consistent panel can still be consistently wrong. This checks that the process was followed, not that the rubric is the right one.
    • Talk time is a proxy. A short answer can be a good one; the number is there to prompt a listen, not to score anybody.
