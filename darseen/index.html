The kitchen

A Kuwaiti cook at the pass, and the world behind her.

Sara Shamsah owns Darseen and runs its kitchen. She is Kuwaiti, and a mother, and the recipes on this menu are the ones she feeds her own family — the machboos rice, the daqoos, the bechamel that comes out on a Friday.

That is the whole standard here. A plate goes out of this kitchen only if she would put it in front of her children.

She means it past closing, too. Hungry at two in the morning? Call her. If she can, she will cook for you there and then — the way a mother feeds her children, who never get hungry at a reasonable hour either.

Behind her is a kitchen of cooks from all over the world — among them Luqman and Mishari, chefs from Bahrain and Qatar — Gulf neighbours who grew up on the same spices and know what the food is supposed to taste like. They bring their own knife work and their own habits, and they cook her recipes to her measure. It is why the food tastes like Kuwait and the kitchen sounds like Manchester.

Every plate leaves under her eye. If a dish is wrong, it goes back — which is the only kitchen rule worth putting on a website.

The menu

What comes to the table

From the rice pot

عيش
  • Machboosمجبوس
    £12Serves 3

    Kuwait's national dish. Rice cooked in the stock of the meat, dark with baharat and loomi — dried black lime — and topped with fried onion. Chicken, lamb or fish. Served with daqoos, the sharp tomato sauce that goes over the rice.

  • Mutabbaq zubaidiمطبق زبيدي
    £20Serves 5–6

    Silver pomfret — zubaidi, the fish Kuwaitis argue about — fried whole and laid over red spiced rice. The dish that tastes most like the Gulf.

From the oven

من الفرن
  • Musakhanمسخن
    £10Serves 2

    Chicken and slow-cooked onion rolled in bread, baked, then buried in sumac until it stains everything purple. Served with a garlic yoghurt to dip. Palestinian by birth, and on every table in the Gulf.

  • Bechamelبشاميل
    £10Serves 2

    Baked pasta layered with spiced mince under a thick béchamel, browned on top. The Friday-lunch dish of Kuwaiti kitchens, and the one children ask for.

Mezze and salads

مقبلات
  • Fattoushفتوش
    £7

    Tomato, cucumber, radish and herbs, sharp with sumac and pomegranate molasses, with toasted khubz broken through it. Order it to cut the rice.

Something sweet

حلو
  • Luqaimatلقيمات
    £7

    Little balls of dough fried until the shell cracks and the inside stays soft, then drenched in dibs — date syrup — and scattered with sesame. Eaten hot, always too many.

To drink

المشروبات
  • Vimtoﭬيمتو
    £4

    The Kuwaiti drink. It is what breaks the fast on every table in Ramadan — poured over ice before anyone touches the food — and we keep it on all year, not only for the month.

  • Awar galbعوار قلب
    £4

    Mango and strawberry blended thick and cold, the fruit streaked through it. A Kuwaiti juice-stall drink whose name means heartache — nobody agrees why.

  • Labanلبن
    £4

    Salted drinking yoghurt, cold enough to hurt. The right answer to a plate of machboos.
